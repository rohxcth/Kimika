# Style anchor: market column sections

The following example shows the expected structure and density for daily market column reports (equities, bonds, currencies, commodities).

---

## Example: Equities section

**Levels and moves**

| Index | Level | 1-day | 1-week |
|---|---|---|---|
| S&P 500 | 5,832 | +0.6% | +1.2% |
| Nasdaq | 18,447 | +0.9% | +1.8% |
| Dow Jones | 43,216 | +0.3% | +0.8% |
| ASX 200 | 8,341 | -0.3% | +0.7% |
| FTSE 100 | 8,112 | +0.2% | -0.4% |
| Nikkei 225 | 39,218 | +1.1% | +2.3% |

**What moved and why**

US markets rose, led by technology after strong earnings from the largest index components. The S&P 500 extended its October gain to 3.2%, its best monthly performance since May. Mega-cap technology contributed approximately 60% of the session's index move; energy and utilities lagged on softer commodity prices.

The ASX 200 slipped 0.3% as materials fell 1.5% on weaker iron ore. The big four banks added a combined 0.4% following the Westpac/BMG acquisition announcement. Healthcare offset some of the materials decline, rising 0.8%.

**Cross-asset context**

A softer USD (DXY -0.4%) and stable US 10-year Treasury yields at 4.38% supported risk appetite broadly. AUD/USD held above 0.645 despite the weaker iron ore print, benefiting from risk-on flows. Gold rose 0.7% to US$2,718/oz on residual safe-haven demand ahead of Friday's US payrolls release.

**Outlook**

Consensus remains constructive into year-end: positioning is light relative to seasonal norms, earnings beats have been broad-based, and rate expectations have stabilised. The key near-term risk is the October US payrolls print; a materially stronger number would push back rate-cut pricing and compress multiples. The forward P/E on the S&P 500 is 21.4x, above the 10-year average of 18.6x, leaving limited room for disappointment.

## Example: Bonds section

**Government bond yields**

| Market | 2y | 10y | 30y | 10y move (1d) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US Treasury | 4.82% | 4.38% | 4.55% | -4bp |
| AU ACGB | 4.14% | 4.42% | 4.71% | -2bp |
| UK Gilt | 4.31% | 4.21% | 4.68% | -3bp |
| German Bund | 2.18% | 2.41% | 2.74% | -2bp |

The US 10-year Treasury yield fell 4bp to 4.38% as softer-than-expected ISM manufacturing data reduced near-term growth concerns and prompted a modest rally in duration. The 2s10s curve steepened 3bp to -44bp, still deeply inverted but moving toward normalisation as markets bring forward the timing of Fed cuts.

The AU 10-year ACGB yield eased 2bp in sympathy to 4.42%, tracking the UST move. The spread to US Treasuries remains at 4bp, near the lower end of the 12-month range of 0-25bp, reflecting broadly aligned easing expectations across the two central banks.
