## Example: Economy section

**United States**

The Fed held the federal funds rate at 5.25-5.50% at the November FOMC meeting, maintaining its data-dependent stance. The updated dot plot implies one 25bp cut in 2026, against market pricing of approximately 40bp of easing, suggesting the committee is leaning against the degree of loosening priced by markets. Core PCE remained at 3.1% year-on-year in September, well above the 2% target; payrolls of +187,000 in October were broadly in line with expectations and consistent with a soft landing narrative. The consensus expects the Fed to begin cutting in mid-2026, with upside risks to the hold period if services inflation re-accelerates or the labour market reheats.

**Australia**

The RBA held the cash rate at 4.35% at its November meeting. The statement acknowledged that inflation was returning to target but retained a mild tightening bias, noting that the board remained alert to upside risks. Trimmed mean CPI eased to 3.5% year-on-year in Q3 2026 - the lowest since 2022 - but remains above the 2-3% target band. Markets are pricing two 25bp cuts by end-2026 (cumulative 50bp), while the median economist forecast implies the first cut no earlier than Q2 2026, with a total of 75bp of easing through the cycle.

**Outlook and scenarios**

Base case (consensus): both the Fed and RBA hold through Q1 2026 before beginning shallow easing cycles; AUD/USD remains range-bound at 0.63-0.66 as rate differentials stay broadly neutral.

Downside: US tariff escalation reignites goods inflation; both central banks hold through 2026; AUD falls toward 0.60 as commodity demand slows and global risk appetite deteriorates. Deal activity contracts as elevated financing costs suppress valuations.

Upside: inflation falls faster than projected; both central banks front-load cuts in H1 2026; yield compression and multiple expansion support deal activity and equity issuance.
